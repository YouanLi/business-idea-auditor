# Business Pitfall Library

Read before each audit. These 12 initial rules are screening hypotheses, not empirically established universal laws. Test applicability to the specific business and jurisdiction. A red flag triggers investigation, not automatic rejection unless a verified hard constraint makes the model infeasible.

## A. Distribution and platform risk

- **RULE-01 — Platform loophole dependency.** A business relying on prohibited automation, copied content or temporary enforcement gaps may lose its distribution overnight. Check current official rules and dependency on a single platform. Do not assume a fixed account lifespan.
- **RULE-02 — Free organic traffic illusion.** Revenue screenshots may omit failed accounts, founder labor and acquisition costs. Ask for cohort-level conversion, attributable acquisition spend and repeatable distribution evidence.
- **RULE-03 — Trust and human interaction.** Products requiring fit, taste, reassurance or complex advice may convert poorly with unattended automation. Compare assisted and automated conversion and support costs; do not assume a universal conversion rate.

## B. Finance and hidden costs

- **RULE-04 — GMV is not profit.** Reconcile gross sales with refunds, fees, advertising, fulfillment, taxes and labor. Missing expenses make profitability unverified, not necessarily low.
- **RULE-05 — Cash-flow squeeze.** Long customer payment terms and upfront supplier payments can exhaust cash even when accounting profit is positive. Model peak working capital and downside exposure before accepting orders.
- **RULE-06 — Low-ticket logistics trap.** Shipping, packaging and returns can erase contribution on low-priced goods. Obtain actual quotes at realistic order volumes and compare regions.

## C. Cross-border, compliance and supply chains

- **RULE-07 — Regulatory prerequisites.** Verify market-specific certification, safety, tax and registration obligations before committing inventory. CE, FCC, UL and VAT have different scopes and are not interchangeable universal requirements.
- **RULE-08 — Undifferentiated resale.** Transparent marketplaces and identical suppliers can compress margins while leaving sellers responsible for returns and service. Test landed costs, differentiation, supplier reliability and actual acquisition costs.

## D. Software, AI and B2B delivery

- **RULE-09 — Native feature substitution.** A thin AI wrapper may lose pricing power when a major platform adds the same feature. Test specialized workflows, integrations, distribution, switching costs and data advantages.
- **RULE-10 — Custom services disguised as automation.** Repeated bespoke configuration, onsite work and support can turn software into a labor-intensive agency. Measure onboarding hours, repeatability and marginal delivery cost; an 80/20 standardization ratio is a heuristic, not a requirement.
- **RULE-11 — Infrequent use and subscription mismatch.** Rare tasks may not support subscriptions. Compare one-time purchases, usage pricing, bundling and acquisition economics.
- **RULE-12 — Stakeholder incentive conflict.** A solution that helps the buyer but increases frontline workload or threatens intermediaries may face adoption resistance. Interview users, approvers and beneficiaries separately; test alternative explanations.
